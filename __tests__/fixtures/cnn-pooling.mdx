# Understanding Convolutional Neural Networks and Pooling Layers

Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) rely heavily on pooling operations to progressively reduce spatial dimensions, minimizing computation and controlling overfitting while maintaining translational invariance.

## Pooling Operations in Modern Architectures

For example, a $2 \times 2$ max-pooling operation with a stride of 2 halves both dimensions, downsampling an input feature map from $4 \times 4$ to $2 \times 2$.

Consider the input activation map $X$ and its pooled representation $P$:

$$ X = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 3 & 2 & 4 \\ 5 & 6 & 1 & 2 \\ 7 & 2 & 8 & 3 \\ 4 & 1 & 5 & 9 \end{bmatrix} $$

$$ P = \begin{bmatrix} 6 & 4 \\ 7 & 9 \end{bmatrix} $$

## Activation and Downsampling Comparison

| Activation | Formula | Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| ReLU | $f(x) = \max(0, x)$ | Piecewise linear, fast gradient flow |
| GELU | $f(x) = x \Phi(x)$ | Smooth probabilistic gating |
| SiLU | $f(x) = x \cdot \sigma(x)$ | Swish self-gated non-linearity |

## PyTorch Pooling Implementation

Here is an efficient PyTorch implementation showing custom pooling logic with tensor slicing:

```python
import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class CustomMaxPool2d(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, kernel_size: int = 2, stride: int = 2):
        super().__init__()
        self.pool = nn.MaxPool2d(kernel_size=kernel_size, stride=stride)

    def forward(self, x: torch.Tensor) -> torch.Tensor:
        # Check boundary shapes where dimension < 2
        if x.shape[-1] < 2 or x.shape[-2] < 2:
            return x
        return self.pool(x)
```

## Global Average Pooling Mathematics

In modern backbones such as ResNet, global average pooling replaces dense fully-connected heads to compute spatial averages:

$$
\text{GAP}(X_k) = \frac{1}{H \times W} \sum_{i=1}^{H} \sum_{j=1}^{W} X_{k, i, j}
$$

Statistical significance checks often yield $p < 0.05$ across validation splits with confidence {0.95, 0.99}.
